    Runs on one Windows office PC, no server room needed

    From paper to searchable archive in four steps

    Your staff scan as they do today. LibraDigit handles cleanup, text recognition and cataloguing.

    1. Scan or import

      Drop in folders of PDFs, TIFFs or JPEGs from any scanner, one page or thousands at a time.

    2. Clean up automatically

      Pages are rotated upright, contrast-enhanced and denoised so faded print and handwriting read clearly.

    3. Recognise and catalogue

      Printed and handwritten text is extracted, staff correct it side by side, and title, author, year and subject are suggested.

    4. Archive and search

      Each record is saved as a searchable PDF/A-2b in a checksummed BagIt package, and the whole collection becomes full-text searchable.

    Student records stay on your campus

    LibraDigit processes and stores everything on hardware you control. Processing and search work fully offline.

    • Password-protected accessA master password set on first launch, stored as a salted bcrypt hash. Files and the search index stay in folders on your machine.
    • Integrity you can verifyEvery archive package carries SHA-256 and MD5 manifests, so you can prove a file hasn't changed in years.
    • Designed for data-protection obligationsKeeping personal data on-site helps you meet India's DPDP Act requirements and your own policies.
    • Zero telemetryNo usage data or analytics is sent anywhere. The optional translate feature, which uses an online service, can be switched off for fully offline use.

    Questions from IT and library teams

    Something else on your mind? Ask us directly.

    What hardware do we need?

    A modern Windows PC is enough. Each office that digitizes runs its own installation. A shared campus server edition is on the roadmap, and we'll recommend a specification during scoping.

    Does it work without an internet connection?

    Yes. OCR, cleanup, storage and search all run locally. The only feature that goes online is optional translation, and it can be turned off.

    Can it read handwritten registers and regional languages?

    Printed text is recognised in the languages installed with Tesseract OCR. Handwriting switches to a dedicated recognition mode, with accuracy typically between 75% and 92% depending on legibility, and staff can correct text side by side. We test your own pages during the demo.

    Will it work with our institutional repository?

    You can export the catalogue as Dublin Core CSV for DSpace or Omeka, or as oai_dc XML. Each record is also saved as a BagIt package with its PDF.

    Do we need to buy new scanners?

    No. It accepts PDFs, TIFFs and JPEGs from the scanners you already have.

    Who owns the digitized data?

    You do. Files sit in an ordinary folder structure (Subject, Year, Title) on your machine, and the metadata travels inside each PDF as XMP.
